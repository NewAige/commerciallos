Detailed Platform Analysis
Abrigo APX
* Pros: Native data integration with the bank's recently deployed Abrigo Risk Rating engine; minimal administrative overhead; fast deployment timelines; intuitive user interface requiring limited Relationship Manager (RM) and Portfolio Manager (PM) training; optimized for community to mid-sized regional banks ($2B–$10B).
* Cons: Less granular customizability in hyper-complex syndicated debt structuring compared to enterprise platforms; reporting modules rely on pre-built templates requiring structured exports for ad-hoc queries.
Global Wave CreditTrack
* Pros: Exceptionally strong credit structuring and spreading engines; fine-grained rule-based workflow engine built specifically for mid-market commercial lending; robust native risk rating tools.
* Cons: Complex interface resulting in steeper learning curve for field RMs; higher implementation costs than Abrigo; integration connectors to COCC and LaserPro require customized API mapping.
nCino
* Pros: Market-leading enterprise workflow engine; deep integration ecosystem; highly scalable dynamic UI; powerful reporting and analytics built on the Salesforce platform.
* Cons: Prohibitive administrative burden requiring dedicated Salesforce administrators; high total cost of ownership; prolonged implementation cycle (12–18 months); significant risk of low RM adoption due to interface density.

	Strategic Recommendation & Risk Justification
Primary Selection: Abrigo APX
Abrigo APX is recommended as the primary Commercial LOS. It offers direct alignment with the bank's existing investment in the Abrigo Risk Rating engine, provides pre-built integration pathways to COCC and LaserPro, and features an intuitive user experience.
Critical Risk Analysis on nCino
While nCino remains a market leader in enterprise banking, selecting nCino introduces severe operational risk for this institution. The platform requires ongoing management by a dedicated, certified Salesforce Administrator. Operating nCino with a part-time administrator leads to unconfigured workflows, low RM adoption, delayed implementation, and excessive reliance on costly third-party consultants.

Strategic Comparison: Abrigo SBL vs. Numerated (Moody's)
Real-Time Credit Decisioning & Scoring Models
* Abrigo SBL: Uses standard scorecards combining SBFE (Small Business Financial Exchange), personal credit scores (FICO), and proprietary bank-defined risk matrices. Fully integrated into the broader Abrigo credit suite.
* Numerated: Advanced data aggregation using pre-fill technology, real-time credit bureau pulls, and automated financial health scoring built on Moody's Analytics credit models.
Plaid & Checking Account Cash Flow Analysis
* Abrigo SBL: Direct integration with Plaid and bank statement parsing tools to analyze real-time cash flow, average daily balances, and overdraft frequencies.
* Numerated: Industry-leading bank transaction analytics; rapidly parses primary transaction account history via Plaid or direct statement uploads to calculate debt service coverage ratios (DSCR) dynamically.

4. Digital Account Opening Strategy: fiVISION Integration Architecture
To support core deposit acquisition alongside commercial asset expansion, the institution requires a streamlined, compliant digital account opening ecosystem.+-----------------------------------------------------------------------+


|                  DIGITAL ACCOUNT OPENING ARCHITECTURE                 |


|                                                                       |


|  [ Customer Portal: fiVISION ]                                        |


|         |                                                             |


|         +---> Core Banking Pipeline  ----> [ COCC Core ]              |


|         +---> Archival Sweep         ----> [ Identifi ECM ]           |


|         +---> Business Credit Handoff---> [ Abrigo APX / SBL ]        |


+-----------------------------------------------------------------------+
High-Level System Architecture
The digital account opening framework positions fiVISION as the customer-facing intake layer, interacting directly with core storage, document repositories, and underwriting engines:


* Inbound Application Layer: fiVISION captures consumer and business deposit applications, performing real-time identity verification (IDV), OFAC screening, and fraud checks.
* Core Processing Engine: COCC acts as the system of record for account creation, customer profile generation, and ledger management via native RESTful APIs.
* Document Management: Identifi automatically indexes, stores, and archives generated signature cards, disclosures, and verification records.
* Credit Cross-Sell Engine: Abrigo APX / SBL receives qualified business applicants directly from fiVISION for streamlined credit underwriting.
Operational Workflow & Cross-Sell Handoff
Customer Application (fiVISION)


  │


  ├── IDV / OFAC / Fraud Check


  │


  ├── Account Provisioned (COCC API)


  │


  ├── Compliance Documents Swept (Identifi ECM)


  │


  └── [OPTIONAL] Business Credit Requested?


        ├── YES ──> Automated Lead/App Handoff ──> Abrigo SBL / APX


        └── NO  ──> Complete Onboarding


1. Intake & Verification: Customer completes applicant workflow in fiVISION. Automated KYB/KYC checks execute instantly.
2. Deposit Account Provisioning: Upon pass validation, fiVISION pushes customer data to COCC to establish the deposit account and core record.
3. Document Archival: Completed signature cards, identification copies, and disclosures pass via automated batch/API sweeps into Identifi ECM, tagged with COCC account numbers.
4. Cross-Sell Credit Handoff: If a business applicant opts to apply for a commercial line of credit or credit card during onboarding, fiVISION transfers the structured entity data, owner profiles, and verified documents directly to Abrigo SBL (or APX) without requiring manual data re-entry.
Four-Phase Implementation Roadmap
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------+


|                        IMPLEMENTATION TIMELINE                        |


|                                                                       |


|  Phase 1: Product Rules & Logic Engine   [ Q1 ]                       |


|  Phase 2: COCC API Integration Mapping   [ Q2 ]                       |

+-----------------------------------------------------------------------+
Phase 1: Product Configuration & Business Rules Setup
* Define product catalogs for consumer and small business deposit accounts.
* Establish automated decisioning logic for KYB/KYC, CIP, and funding limits within fiVISION.
Phase 2: COCC Core API Integration
* Map bi-directional data fields between fiVISION and COCC.
* Execute end-to-end sandbox testing for real-time account creation, funding, and ledger posting.
Phase 3: Identifi ECM Archival Automation
* Configure automated indexing rules to push signed documents into Identifi.
* Validate document retrievals and audit trails against compliance standards.
Phase 4: Abrigo Credit Cross-Sell Handoff
* Establish data-sharing protocols between fiVISION and Abrigo APX/SBL.
* Enable one-click small business credit requests during the deposit opening process.
Compliance & Administrative Considerations
* Beneficial Ownership Information (BOI) & FinCEN Regulations: The fiVISION workflow automatically collects, verifies, and records beneficial ownership structures (25%+ ownership threshold and control person requirements) prior to account activation, creating an audited record for compliance officers.
* CFPB Section 1071 Data Collection: The small business intake portal incorporates mandatory Section 1071 demographic data collection fields (minority-owned, women-owned, small business designations) using isolated compliance storage to maintain credit decisioning neutrality.
* Low Administrative Overhead Model: By deploying pre-built integration interfaces between fiVISION, COCC, Identifi, and Abrigo, the bank avoids managing custom middleware. This architecture allows the part-time technology administrator to manage system parameters without requiring external development resources.
